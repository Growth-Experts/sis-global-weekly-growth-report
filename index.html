What this means for Connect Mobile

Connect Mobile has invested in content — over 30 blog posts — and has strong services worth ranking for. But two critical technical issues mean Google is navigating the site half-blind. The robots.txt file serves a challenge page instead of crawl instructions, and four separate versions of the site exist simultaneously with no redirects to consolidate them. Every page is being seen as duplicate content. These aren't minor tuning issues — they're foundational problems that suppress everything else. The good news: they're fixable within days.

10 key findings

What we found. What it costs you.

Each finding is graded by its real-world impact on organic visibility, lead generation, and Google's ability to index and rank your pages. We've ordered them by urgency, not complexity.

01
Robots.txt is serving a challenge page, not crawl directives

When Googlebot requests robots.txt — the file that tells it how to crawl your site — it receives a JavaScript spinner page ("One moment, please…") instead. This introduces unpredictable crawl behaviour. Google may default to "allow all" after repeated failures, meaning you have zero control over what gets indexed.

Fix: Whitelist search engine bots from the firewall/CDN challenge layer. Serve a valid plain-text robots.txt. Add a sitemap directive. Estimated: 1–2 hours.
Critical
02
Four duplicate versions of the site — no canonical domain

http, https, www, and non-www all return a 200 OK response with no redirects between them. Google sees four identical copies of every single page on your site. Link equity is split four ways. Crawl budget is wasted. This is one of the most damaging technical SEO issues a site can have.

Fix: Choose one canonical version (https://connect-mobile.co.za). Implement 301 redirects from all others. Add HSTS header. Estimated: 2–4 hours.
Critical
03
30+ blog posts are invisible to Google — sitemap excludes all of them

The sitemap only lists 17 static pages. Every blog post — a significant content investment — is missing. The blog's own sitemap returns a 500 server error. Google has no efficient pathway to discover, crawl, or rank any of this content. It may as well not exist.

Fix: Repair WordPress blog sitemap (likely a plugin config issue). Create a sitemap index combining main site + blog. Submit to Google Search Console.
High
04
The "Contact Us" link in your main navigation returns a 404

The navigation links to /contact-us, but the actual page is at /contact. Every visitor who clicks "Contact Us" lands on a dead page. For a B2B site where contact = leads, this is a direct revenue leak — and it signals to Google that the site is poorly maintained.

Fix: Update the nav link to /contact. Add a 301 redirect from /contact-us as a safety net. Estimated: 15 minutes.
High
05
Homepage title tag contains zero target keywords

The homepage — your highest-authority page — is titled simply "Connect Mobile Communications." No mention of Bulk SMS, mobile messaging, short codes, or USSD. The title tag is the single strongest on-page ranking signal. This is the most impactful quick fix available.

Fix: Rewrite to: "Bulk SMS & Mobile Messaging Solutions | Connect Mobile". Estimated: 10 minutes.
High
06
Blog URLs use a broken double-path structure

All blog post URLs contain /blog/blog/slug/ — a duplication caused by a WordPress permalink misconfiguration. Beyond looking wrong, this creates unnecessarily deep URLs that affect crawl efficiency. Some slugs also use underscores instead of hyphens, which Google doesn't treat as word separators.

Fix: Reconfigure WordPress permalink settings. Implement 301 redirects from old URLs. Standardise on hyphens for new content.
High
07
Blog and service pages are completely siloed — no internal linking

The Bulk SMS service page doesn't link to any of the 5+ blog posts about bulk SMS. Blog posts about short codes don't link to the Short Codes service page. This internal linking gap means link authority isn't flowing between your content and your commercial pages — directly suppressing service page rankings.

Fix: Build a topical cluster linking strategy. Add "Related articles" to service pages. Add service CTAs to blog posts. Ongoing work.
High
08
Google can't verify who you are — weak E-E-A-T signals throughout

Blog posts display an email address as the author, not a real person. There are no customer testimonials anywhere on the site. No FAQ schema. No structured data. In a competitive B2B space, Google's quality raters look for clear expertise and trust signals — both are largely absent here.

Fix: Add author names and bios. Add testimonials section. Implement Organisation, FAQ, and Service schema markup. Ongoing.
Medium
09
The brand name is spelled four different ways — including a typo

The site uses "Connect Mobile Communications", "Connect Mobile Communication", "Connect Mobile", and — in the footer copyright — "Connet IT Systems" (misspelled). Google builds a knowledge graph entity around your brand. Inconsistent naming across pages makes this harder, weakening branded search authority.

Fix: Standardise to one official brand name sitewide. Fix the footer typo. Update LinkedIn URL. Low effort, meaningful signal improvement.
Medium
10
Significant content gaps vs. what competitors likely have

No pricing page (only a downloadable PDF). No comparison pages targeting competitor keywords. No glossary for terms like USSD, SMPP, and RCS. No FAQ sections on service pages (missing schema markup opportunity). No individual industry landing pages. These are proven organic traffic drivers that don't currently exist on the site.

Fix: Content strategy and production. Dedicated pricing page. Comparison content. Industry-specific landing pages. FAQ schema. Long-term project.
Medium
